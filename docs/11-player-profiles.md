# 11 — Player profiles (the people in the footage)

Added 2026-08-21. A Dixie club player asked for a club-wide leaderboard /
profile system, and the Discord thread put it sharper: "if you know you're
going to play against one of these players, is there something your system
might be able to tell you beforehand?" This is the first step — one profile
per named player, built from every clip they were tagged on, on the website.

Not to be confused with `/profile` (`web/src/pages/Profile.tsx`), which is the
signed-in user's OWN coaching profile that the AI coach reads.

## Shape

```
read-out (/analyze, /library) ── "Name this player" on Player A / Player B
        │                                   │
        │ summarizeClip(analysis, side)     │ players + player_clips (Supabase, RLS)
        ▼                                   ▼
   ClipSummary (a few KB: shots, placement, coverage, T-time,
   predictability, shot classes, cell→cell transitions, opponent's numbers)
        │
        ▼
   /players/:id ── buildProfileStats(clips) → stats, scoutingNotes(stats), calendarWeeks(clips)
```

- The **analysis itself never moves.** It stays where it always was — in the
  browser that ran it (`web/src/analysis/history.ts`), footage and pose track
  included. A tag stores only the summary, so a profile is readable from any
  machine without ever uploading a match video anywhere new.
- **Everything is the user's own.** `players` and `player_clips` carry
  `user_id`, and every policy is `auth.uid() = user_id` (authenticated role
  only). Rosters are private to whoever built them; club-wide sharing is a
  later, deliberate step, not an accident waiting to happen.
- **Predictability is pooled the pipeline's way.** The per-clip score is
  `1 − H/Hmax` over the player's cell-to-next-cell transition matrix
  (`entropy_predictability` in `analysis/analyze.py`). The profile sums the
  transition matrices across clips and runs the same formula
  (`predictabilityFromTransitions`), so a profile's score means exactly what a
  single read-out's does — it is not an average of scores. T-time and coverage
  are time-weighted; rally length is rally-weighted; placement and shot mix are
  sums.

## What the profile claims, and on what

The product's rule everywhere else applies here with extra force, because a
profile is read as a verdict on a person:

- **Measured from position and timing** (solid): coverage, time within 1.5 m
  of the T, which court cell a shot went to ("placement" — the pipeline's
  retrieval proxy: the opponent's position when they played the next ball,
  standing in for where the shot landed, because there is no ball tracking),
  the cell-to-cell patterns and the predictability score, rally lengths.
  Shot *detection* was audited at ~63% precision on club footage, so counts
  carry some phantom shots and misses.
- **Indicative only**: the shot classes (drive / cross-court / drop / boast /
  volley) — read from body pose alone and never audited.

Every scouting note carries a basis chip saying which of the two it rests on,
and the notes stay silent under 40 of the player's own shots (30 classified
shots for the class-based ones) rather than extrapolate. The thresholds live
in `scoutingNotes` in one place, each with the number the sentence rests on in
the sentence, so a reader can disagree with the adjective and keep the
measurement.

## The recordings grid

The GitHub-contributions idea from the thread: one cell per day over the last
52 weeks, a recording lights its day, more recordings on a day = a darker
cell, and each cell's tooltip lists the recordings. Levels are relative to the
busiest day so a profile with four recordings still lights up; the grid
scrolls inside its own container on a phone rather than widening the page.

## The first profile

The plumbing test was a "Mohamed ElShorbagy" profile in the owner's roster
built from the four archive.org club matches as clearly-labelled stand-ins (the
player's notes say so). No PSA footage was downloaded — it is copyrighted
broadcast content; real clips, when there are any, replace the stand-ins by
tagging them on their read-outs. `/players/sample` carries the same four clips
under "Sample player" for visitors who are not signed in.

  an opponent's profile is the obvious next step.
